# Horizon panoramas in the 3D view - design

## Intent

The owner saw an AI-retouched frame of our 3D view (the YouTube preview of «Ragnarok Online in 3D»: snowy peaks,
pine forests, a wide sky) and wants the game itself to look like that: «я хочу панорамы горизонта в клиенте
красивые», «я хочу в клиенте сделать, делай». It is for the players and for the promotion the owner is starting on
YouTube and RateMyServer - the horizon is what a 3D-view video or screenshot shows most.

Today the 3D view's horizon is procedural and flat: `sky3d_ps` / `skyAlong` (src/win/gpu/shaders.cpp) paint the sky
where nothing is drawn, and `skyRanges` adds three layers of noise crests (view3d::Ranges: mountains, dunes, snowy
peaks, low islands) - pale silhouettes with no light or detail. Past the map's edge Direct3D 12 also draws land with
hills (core/outskirts, D3D12 only). Both renderers draw the sky (haze.cpp skyPass, renderer11 applySky3d).

## Decided by the owner (19.11)

- Beautiful horizons in the client itself.
- «я хочу чтобы ты нарисовал это на видеокарте, не генерировал с помощью ИИ готовое» - the graphics card draws the
  mountains from formulas; no ready-made pictures, no AI art.
- Approach 1 of three (below): the card bakes a 360° panorama of procedural terrain when a region is entered; the sky
  lights it every frame.

## Approaches (GPU-drawn)

| | What | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **1 (picked)** | At a region's entry the card ray-marches procedural terrain (eroded ridges, snow, forests, rock, the region's own climate) into a 360° strip - its colour, normal, the sun's shadow and distance - over a few frames; each frame the sky shader samples it and lights it by the sun, the night, the dusk and the haze | Beautiful and nearly free per frame; both renderers; no parallax for the far mountains (barely any in life), D3D12's outskirts hills stay in front |
| 2 | The same terrain ray-marched in the sky shader every frame | Exact light at any moment, but too heavy for weak cards (the R7 M370 laptops) without cutting the look |
| 3 | Real mountain meshes past the map's edge (grow outskirts, port them to D3D11) | Honest parallax, but procedural meshes rarely look «like the picture»; heavier; a long port |

(An earlier approach A - painted 360° pictures per region, generated by an AI - was dropped by the owner's word.)

## Goals

- On every outdoor map of a known region (core/region_look's 20: Alberta, Amatsu, Ayothaya, Comodo, Einbroch, Geffen,
  Glast Heim, Hugel, Juno, Kunlun, Lighthalzen, Louyang, Lutie, Morroc, Niflheim, Payon, Prontera, Rachel, Umbala,
  Veins) the 3D view shows mountains, hills, deserts or seas all round, in that region's climate, fixed to the world.
- They look like a landscape, not a silhouette: ridges and valleys eroded, rock on steep slopes, grass and forest on
  gentle ones, snow above a line by height and slope, the sun's light and shadows, distance into the haze.
- Our sky stays alive: clouds, the sun, the stars, the moon, the dawn glow; the sun sets behind the ridges; by night
  dark mountains under the stars; the haze colour of `view3d::skyOf` takes their feet.
- Direct3D 11 and Direct3D 12 alike; the R7 M370 class of card keeps its frame rate (a cheaper bake level for weak
  cards).
- No hitch at a map's entry: the bake is split over frames; until it is done the procedural ranges show.
- A region with no climate of its own (unknown) gets the default (green hills and far mountains).

## Non-goals (this design)

- Parallax layers, real mountain meshes (approach 3).
- Indoors, dungeons (the vault), maps hung in the sky (view3d::hungMap) - none of them see a horizon.
- The classic view from above (no horizon in sight).
- Seasons (a winter climate for Prontera) - later, as a climate variant.
- The water's reflection of the panorama in v1 (builder's call, below).

## Anti-goals

- Anything drawn by an AI or shipped as a picture - the look comes from formulas on the card.
- A frame-rate cost the player can feel, or a stutter at a map's entry.
- A look that fights the game: the colours follow the region's look (core/region_look tints) and the map's own light.

## Constraints

- Shaders compile with FXC on players' PCs: check with `dxc -HV 2018`; loops with fixed upper bounds ([loop]).
- Direct3D 11 feature level 11_0 (pixel shaders 5.0; no compute needed - the bake is a pixel pass into the strip).
- New visual effects ship OFF by default (the owner's restrained-look rule) - see Open questions.
- Settings: [view3d] section, Alt+O «3D-режим» page, strings uk/ru/en; the D3D12 renderer is uaro-render64.exe: a new
  effects field crosses the bridge (remote.cpp writes, serve.cpp reads, protocol bumped) as `skyRanges` did.
- Each change a beta (3.0.22-beta.N); builds `nice -n 19 ionice -c3`, `-j4`.

## The design

### Climates (core, tested)

- `horizon::Climate climateOf(region)`: a table of the 20 regions + a default. A climate is plain numbers: the land's
  kind (hills, mountains, alps, desert with dunes and mesas, sea with islands, karst pillars, volcanic and canyon,
  wasteland), a seed, the height scale and roughness, the ridges' sharpness, the snow line and the tree line, the
  colours (grass, forest, rock, snow, sand, water), how far the land starts (past the map's edge) and ends.
- Suggested climates (tuned on previews with the owner): Prontera green hills with forests and far snowy peaks;
  Payon forested mountains; Geffen hills and cliffs; Morroc dunes and mesas; Alberta and Comodo sea with islands;
  Lutie alps under snow; Louyang karst pillars; Amatsu steep green hills; Niflheim grey dead mountains in fog; Rachel
  and Veins canyon reds; Einbroch and Lighthalzen hills in smog; Umbala and Ayothaya jungle hills; Hugel rolling
  hills; Juno and Kunlun hung in the sky (none); Glast Heim dark ridges.
- A CPU twin of the terrain and material functions (core/horizon_terrain) mirrors the shader's - for the tests (the
  same height and colour at the same point within tolerance), for the previews (tools: a host program writing PNGs of
  a climate as the 3D camera sees it) and for tuning before a beta.

### The bake (the card, per region)

- A strip of 4096 x 384 texels (weak cards 2048 x 192) covering the full 360° and «rise» (the sine of the elevation,
  as skyAlong uses it) from -0.05 to 0.35; stored as two RGBA8 layers (one SRV: a 2-slice array): layer 0 the colour
  and the coverage (alpha 0 where the ray met no land - the sky), layer 1 the normal (octahedral, 2 x 8 bits), the
  sun's visibility (soft shadow) and the distance (8-bit, log).
- A pixel pass over the strip: each texel a ray from the map's middle a little above its ground, marched over the
  climate's height field from the land's start out to its end (adaptive steps; 128 at most, 64 on weak cards), then the
  normal by differences, the material by height/slope/noise, the ambient occlusion from the height around, the sun's
  shadow by a short march toward the map's sun (`mapSun`; 24 steps, none on weak cards). The map's sun is fixed per
  map, so the shadows stay right until the next region.
- Split over frames: an eighth of the strip a frame (rows or column blocks), so a region is ready in a few frames;
  the previous region's strip, or the procedural ranges, show until then. Baked again only when the climate changes
  (region change), the level changes, or the device is made anew.
- The noise: hash-based value/gradient noise in the shader (no texture), the same functions in core.

### Each frame (the sky)

- The sky passes (`sky3d_ps`, `sky3d_haze_ps`) sample the strip with `SampleLevel` (level from the frame's focal
  length; u wraps, v clamps) where `skyRanges` is drawn today; `skyParts` gains a bit (128: «the panorama is there»);
  the ranges are not drawn then.
- Light: albedo x (the sky's ambient by the night + the sun's colour x max(0, N.L) x the baked shadow); the dawn/dusk
  glow on the sun's side; by night a cold dim silhouette; then the haze by the baked distance toward the horizon
  colour (aerial perspective) - the feet palest, so the map's own land and the D3D12 outskirts meet it softly.
- Mountains in front of the clouds, the sun and the stars (composited after them by the coverage).

### The setting

- `[view3d] panorama` - «Панорама обрію» / «Панорама горизонта» / «Horizon panorama», a hint saying what it draws and
  that the card draws it at a region's entry. Default: off (the rule) unless the owner says otherwise. A change applies
  at once (live config); `[view3d] ranges` stays for when it is off.
- Log: «horizon: <map> - the <region> climate baked (WxH, N steps, N frames, N ms on the card)» / «... none: <why>».

## Phases (betas)

1. core: climates + the CPU twin + the preview tool; previews of a few climates shown to the owner (no beta yet).
2. Direct3D 12: the bake pass, the strip, the sky's sampling and light, the setting over the bridge, the log.
3. Direct3D 11: the same; the weak-card level.
4. Tuning by region with the owner's screenshots; the water's reflection; seasons if wanted.

## Left to the builder

- The noise functions, the erosion trick, the material rules and every tuning number (decided on previews).
- Rows vs column blocks for the split; how the 2-slice array binds in each renderer's sky passes (a free slot).
- Whether the water's reflection samples the strip in v1.
- The exact weak-card rule (the card level the project already measures).
